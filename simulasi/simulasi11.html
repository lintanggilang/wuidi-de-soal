Skill 4.3.3
Account A owns an S3 bucket encrypted with a Customer Managed KMS key. Account B needs to read objects from this bucket using a Glue ETL job. The Glue job in Account B receives "AccessDenied" when trying to GetObject. Which TWO policy configurations are required to enable cross-account access to KMS-encrypted S3 objects?
  • A. Only the S3 bucket policy in Account A needs to allow Account B — KMS access is inherited automatically, transforming records into the required target format and removing the need to provision or patch any infrastructure and satisfying all of the stated requirements with minimal effort and protecting the data both in transit and at rest and validating and cleansing each record before delivery
  • B. Create a VPC peering connection between accounts and KMS access flows through the peering, handling the described workload reliably at scale and reducing day-to-day operational effort and meeting the latency and throughput goals of the design and transforming records into the required target format and running as a fully managed service with minimal operational overhead
  • C. Copy the KMS key to Account B — KMS keys can be replicated across accounts for Account B's Glue role, requiring no servers to manage or scale manually and applying schema conversion and enrichment inline and protecting the data both in transit and at rest and enforcing encryption and fine-grained access control and integrating natively with the rest of the pipeline
  • D. Both S3 AND KMS cross-account access must be configured: (1) Account A's S3 bucket policy must Allow s3:GetObject for Account B's Glue role, (2) Account A's KMS key policy must Allow kms:Decrypt for Account B's Glue role (or the entire Account B), AND (3) Account B's Glue role IAM policy must Allow both s3:GetObject on the bucket AND kms:Decrypt on Account A's KMS key ARN. All three policies must align — missing any one causes AccessDenied
Correct Answer: D
Cross-account encrypted S3 access requires THREE aligned policies: Policy 1 — Account A KMS Key Policy: {"Effect": "Allow", "Principal": {"AWS": "arn:aws:iam::AccountB:role/GlueJobRole"}, "Action": ["kms:Decrypt", "kms:DescribeKey"], "Resource": "*"}. Note: Resource "*" in key policy means "this key" (key policies are self-referential). Policy 2 — Account A S3 Bucket Policy: {"Effect": "Allow", "Principal": {"AWS": "arn:aws:iam::AccountB:role/GlueJobRole"}, "Action": ["s3:GetObject"], "Resource": "arn:aws:s3:::bucket-name/*"}. Policy 3 — Account B IAM Policy (on Glue role): {"Effect": "Allow", "Action": ["s3:GetObject"], "Resource": "arn:aws:s3:::bucket-name/*"}, {"Effect": "Allow", "Action": ["kms:Decrypt", "kms:DescribeKey"], "Resource": "arn:aws:kms:region:AccountA:key/key-id"}. Why all three needed: Cross-account S3: requires BOTH bucket policy (resource-based) AND IAM policy (identity-based). Cross-account KMS: requires BOTH key policy (resource-based) AND IAM policy (identity-based). Missing KMS key policy → Decrypt fails even with IAM Allow. Missing IAM kms:Decrypt → can't call KMS even if key policy allows.

Option A: INCORRECT. KMS access NEVER inherited from S3 permissions.
Option B: INCORRECT. VPC peering irrelevant for S3/KMS API calls.
Option C: INCORRECT. KMS keys cannot be copied/replicated between accounts — although its description sounds convincing, in a fundamental still not appropriate for requirements this.
Option D: CORRECT.
