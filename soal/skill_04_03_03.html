Cross-account encrypted S3 access requires THREE aligned policies: Policy 1 — Account A KMS Key Policy: {"Effect": "Allow", "Principal": {"AWS": "arn:aws:iam::AccountB:role/GlueJobRole"}, "Action": ["kms:Decrypt", "kms:DescribeKey"], "Resource": "*"}. Policy 2 — Account A S3 Bucket Policy: {"Effect": "Allow", "Principal": {"AWS": "arn:aws:iam::AccountB:role/GlueJobRole"}, "Action": ["s3:GetObject"], "Resource": "arn:aws:s3:::bucket-name/*"}. Why all three needed: Cross-account S3: requires BOTH bucket policy (resource-based) AND IAM policy (identity-based). Cross-account KMS: requires BOTH key policy (resource-based) AND IAM policy (identity-based). Missing KMS key policy → Decrypt fails even with IAM Allow. Missing IAM kms:Decrypt → can't call KMS even if key policy allows.

When to use Grants vs Key Policy: Use GRANTS when: Access is temporary (project-based, time-limited). Programmatic management needed (automation creates/retires). Many consumers (avoids key policy size limit). Service integration (AWS services use grants automatically). Use KEY POLICY when: Permanent, stable access (organizational access). Small number of principals. Want immediate consistency (grants are eventually consistent). Need to see all permissions in one document.
