Jawaban Benar: D
Crypto-shredding adalah approach yang paling practical dan reliable untuk GDPR deletion: Architecture: (1) During data ingestion: Each customer gets unique KMS key (or per-customer data key encrypted by a customer-specific CMK). PII fields encrypted with customer's key: {name: ENC(key_123, "John Doe"), email: ENC(key_123, "john@example.com"), transactions: [unencrypted aggregate data]}. ALL encrypted PII across S3, Redshift, DynamoDB → permanently unreadable. Aggregated/anonymized data derived from customer remains usable. Advantages over physical deletion: Don't need to find every copy across distributed systems (backups, caches, logs). Instant "logical deletion" — data becomes garbage without key. Works even for data in immutable storage (S3 Glacier, backups).

Opsi A: SALAH. Moving data isn't deleting it — meskipun deskripsinya terdengar meyakinkan, secara fundamental tetap tidak sesuai untuk kebutuhan ini.

A data science team needs access to production customer data for model training but must comply with HIPAA requirements. The data includes patient diagnoses, ages, and zip codes. Simply removing names and SSNs is not sufficient — the team must ensure individuals cannot be re-identified from combinations of quasi-identifiers. Which technique addresses this?

  • B. Apply k-anonymity through generalization — generalize quasi-identifiers so each record is indistinguishable from at least k-1 other records: generalize age to ranges (e.g., 30-39), truncate zip codes to 3-digit prefix (e.g., 981**), group rare diagnoses into broader categories. This prevents re-identification through linkage attacks on combinations of quasi-identifiers
  • C. Remove only the direct identifiers (name, SSN, phone) and share the rest unchanged, satisfying all of the stated requirements with minimal effort and meeting the latency and throughput goals of the design and integrating natively with the rest of the pipeline and handling the described workload reliably at scale
Jawaban Benar: B
K-anonymity via generalization addresses re-identification risk: Problem: Direct identifiers removed, but quasi-identifiers (age, zip, diagnosis) combined can uniquely identify individuals. Example: "35-year-old male, zip 98115, diabetes Type-1" → might identify only one person in a small community. K-anonymity (k≥5 for HIPAA Safe Harbor): Generalization: Age 35 → "30-39". Zip 98115 → "981**". Rare diagnosis → broader category. Result: Every record matches at least k-1 other records — cannot pinpoint individual. HIPAA Safe Harbor method requires: Zip codes truncated to 3 digits (or 000 if population < 20,000). Ages above 89 grouped as "90+". Dates generalized to year only. 18 specified identifiers removed or generalized. Implementation in ETL: Glue PySpark: bin ages, truncate zips, apply generalization hierarchies. Verify k value: GROUP BY generalized quasi-identifiers, HAVING COUNT(*) >= k for all groups.

Opsi A: SALAH. Random noise destroys data utility for ML; diagnosis noise makes data useless — meskipun deskripsinya terdengar meyakinkan, secara fundamental tetap tidak sesuai untuk kebutuhan ini.
Opsi B: BENAR.
Opsi C: SALAH. Encryption ≠ anonymization under HIPAA; encrypted data is still considered PHI.
Opsi D: SALAH. Insufficient, quasi-identifier linkage attacks still possible.

A data engineering team applies SHA-256 hashing to email addresses for anonymization in their data lake. A security auditor flags this approach as insufficient for true anonymization. What is the auditor's concern, and what technique should supplement or replace simple hashing?
  • A. Plain SHA-256 hashing of emails is vulnerable to dictionary/rainbow table attacks — an attacker can hash all common email patterns (firstname.lastname@domain.com) and match against the hashed values to recover original emails. The solution: use HMAC with a secret key (keyed hashing) or apply salted hashing, making rainbow table attacks infeasible since the attacker would need the secret key or per-record salt to reproduce hashes
  • B. SHA-256 is a broken algorithm and should be replaced with MD5 for better security and match against the hashed values to recover original emails, enforcing encryption and fine-grained access control and handling the described workload reliably at scale and satisfying strict compliance and audit requirements and protecting the data both in transit and at rest and satisfying all of the stated requirements with minimal effort
  • C. SHA-256 produces output that is too long to store efficiently in databases, integrating natively with the rest of the pipeline and enforcing encryption and fine-grained access control and meeting the latency and throughput goals of the design and satisfying all of the stated requirements with minimal effort and satisfying strict compliance and audit requirements
  • D. The auditor's concern is that SHA-256 is reversible and the original email can be mathematically derived from the hash, enforcing encryption and fine-grained access control and satisfying strict compliance and audit requirements and integrating natively with the rest of the pipeline and satisfying all of the stated requirements with minimal effort and meeting the latency and throughput goals of the design
Jawaban Benar: A
Plain hashing BUKAN true anonymization untuk low-entropy data seperti email: Vulnerability: Email addresses have limited entropy — predictable patterns. Attacker approach: Generate list of probable emails (firstname.lastname combinations + common domains). Hash each: SHA256("john.smith@gmail.com") → "abc123...". Match against hashed dataset. Result: millions of emails recoverable in minutes. This is a "pre-image attack via enumeration" — technically SHA-256 is one-way, but the INPUT SPACE is small enough to enumerate. Solutions (in order of preference): (1) HMAC with secret key: HMAC-SHA256(secret_key, email) — without secret_key, rainbow tables useless. Key management critical — store in KMS/Secrets Manager. (2) Salted hash: SHA256(random_salt + email) — different salt per record. Prevents matching across records but salt must be stored. (3) Differential privacy: Add calibrated noise to aggregated statistics rather than pseudonymizing individual records. For true anonymization: Consider if you need consistency (HMAC/tokenization) or irreversibility (generalization, differential privacy).

Opsi A: BENAR.
Opsi B: SALAH. MD5 is LESS secure than SHA-256, factually wrong — meskipun deskripsinya terdengar meyakinkan, secara fundamental tetap tidak sesuai untuk kebutuhan ini.
Opsi C: SALAH. 64-character hex output is standard database column size.
Opsi D: SALAH. SHA-256 is NOT mathematically reversible; the issue is enumeration attack on limited input space.
